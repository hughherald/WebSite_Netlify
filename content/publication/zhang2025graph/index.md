abstract: Significant intra-individual variability and inter-individual differences in scalp electroencephalography (EEG) make it difficult to learn task-discriminative features for motor imagery brain-computer interfaces. This paper proposes graph-based feature learning methods for both subject-dependent and subject-independent motor imagery EEG decoding. A complete time-frequency-spatial-graph (TFSG) feature space is constructed by segmenting EEG into multiple time-frequency units with filter banks and sliding windows, then fusing spatial and brain-network graph features. Two discriminative learning methods are further developed, including a nonconvex sparse optimization model with log-function regularization and a Fisher-criterion-regularized sparse model, under a unified optimization framework. Experiments on two motor imagery EEG datasets show strong performance, with average classification accuracies of 82.93%, 68.52%, and 71.69% under subject-dependent, subject-independent, and subject-adaptive evaluations.
