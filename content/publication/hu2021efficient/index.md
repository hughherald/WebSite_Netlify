abstract: Emotion plays an essential role in human health and daily life. In this paper, EEG microstate analysis is introduced for dynamic analysis of video-evoked emotions. A sequential clustering process is proposed for validated and representative microstate detection in emotion-related EEG dynamics characterization, and the underlying neural activation patterns under different emotion states are explored. The results demonstrate that EEG microstates extracted from the proposed sequential clustering are discriminative for dynamic emotion analysis. An increased activation of MS2 and MS4 but decreased activation of MS3 is found after emotion induction. Distinct emotional-level effects for valence and arousal are also observed. This work validates the possibility of applying EEG microstate analysis for emotion-related neural mechanism investigation and supports EEG microstate analysis as a powerful tool for exploring spatiotemporal brain changes during emotion perception.
