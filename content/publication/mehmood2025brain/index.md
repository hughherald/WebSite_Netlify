abstract: "Non-suicidal self-injury (NSSI) is a growing public health concern whose neurobiological mechanisms in adolescents remain poorly understood. A sample of 89 adolescents (46 NSSI positive, 43 NSSI negative) aged 15.39 ± 1.77 years was recruited, and resting-state fMRI was used to examine whole-brain connectivity patterns alongside assessments of psychological resilience and NSSI behavior. The NSSI positive group showed significantly lower resilience scores. Connectivity between sensorimotor and limbic networks was negatively associated with NSSI, while sensorimotor–subcortical connectivity showed a positive association. Stronger connectivity between the dorsal attention and default mode networks indirectly reduced NSSI by enhancing psychological resilience. These findings highlight specific brain connectivity patterns and psychological resilience as key targets for neurobiologically informed interventions in vulnerable adolescents."
