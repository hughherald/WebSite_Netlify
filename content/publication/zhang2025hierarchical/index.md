abstract: Time-frequency-spatial features are essential for motor imagery EEG classification, but effectively leveraging their multidimensional structure remains challenging. This study proposes a Hierarchical Feature Recalibration Network (HFRNet) that explicitly models intergroup dependencies and hierarchical relationships among features. The method uses a two-layer feature-recalibration mechanism with squeeze-and-excitation operations to reweight feature maps first at the time-frequency-unit level and then at the time-window level, followed by a classification module. Experiments on five motor imagery datasets show strong overall performance and robust generalization compared with multiple feature-selection baselines and recent state-of-the-art methods.
