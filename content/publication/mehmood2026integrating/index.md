abstract: Integrating electroencephalography (EEG) and functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) with naturalistic stimuli has advanced understanding of neural mechanisms underlying mental disorders. This review synthesizes studies from 2014 to 2024 on anxiety, depression, and schizophrenia in naturalistic paradigms, highlighting common and disorder-specific alterations across affective and cognitive networks. Anxiety is associated with hyperactivity in the amygdala, prefrontal cortex, anterior cingulate cortex, and insula, with changes in dorsal attention, default mode, and frontoparietal networks. Depression shows disruptions in default mode and frontoparietal connectivity and altered amygdala-prefrontal interactions, while schizophrenia involves abnormalities in motor and language processing networks across frontal, parietal, and temporal regions including the insula and temporoparietal junction. The findings support naturalistic paradigms as a promising direction for more personalized diagnostics and interventions in mental health.
