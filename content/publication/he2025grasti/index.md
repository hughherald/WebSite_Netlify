abstract: Resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging (rs-fMRI) has shown growing potential as a noninvasive tool for assisting clinical diagnosis of brain disorders, but constructing functional connectivity networks that jointly capture spatial and temporal information remains challenging. This paper proposes Graph Spatial-Temporal Infomax (GraSTI), a graph augmentation strategy based on the information bottleneck principle that adaptively preserves global spatial functional connectivity and local temporal dynamics. GraSTI is further integrated into an adversarial contrastive learning framework to balance representation effectiveness and robustness for downstream diagnosis tasks. Evaluations on datasets of Alzheimer's disease, major depressive disorder, and bipolar disorder demonstrate consistent gains over existing methods, with notable accuracy improvements across all three disorders and interpretable identification of disorder-related brain regions and connections.
