abstract: Continuous electroencephalography (EEG) signals are widely used in affective brain-computer interface applications, but only a subset of continuously recorded data is truly relevant to emotion processing while the rest can be noisy or unrelated. This work proposes Emotion Agent, an unsupervised deep reinforcement learning framework that automatically identifies informative emotional segments from continuous EEG. The method first performs heuristic global search to generate prototype representations, then uses a distribution-prototype-based reward to encourage selection of representative and emotion-relevant segments, and is optimized with proximal policy optimization for stable convergence. Experiments on three benchmark datasets for both discrete and dimensional emotion recognition show an average performance improvement of 13.46%, demonstrating improved accuracy and robustness for downstream aBCI tasks.
