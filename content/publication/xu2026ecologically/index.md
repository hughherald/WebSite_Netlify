abstract: Mood disorders, including major depressive disorder (MDD) and bipolar disorder (BD), are prevalent conditions characterized by persistent emotional dysregulation and substantial functional impairment, yet reliable neurofunctional markers remain limited. This study proposes Divergent Emotional Functional Networks (DEFN), derived from fMRI under naturalistic emotional contexts by integrating naturalistic emotion induction, dynamic functional connectivity, and machine learning. Emotion-specific functional patterns in healthy individuals were identified with 83.99% accuracy and then validated in clinical cohorts, including multi-site MDD datasets and an independent BD dataset. Using static functional connectivity with nested 10-fold cross-validation, DEFN-based models achieved stronger classification performance than baseline models for both MDD and BD, while maintaining reproducibility across age and sex. These findings suggest DEFN as a robust and clinically relevant neural marker to improve mood disorder diagnostics and support earlier intervention.
