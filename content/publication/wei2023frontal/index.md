abstract: Prestimulus occipital alpha power is known to influence visual perceptual decision-making, but whether top-down frontal-occipital phase synchronization modulates this occipital alpha power remains unclear. This study combined general linear mixed model, event-related potential analysis, and both univariate and multivariate phase-synchronization analyses to investigate this mechanism in a visual detection task. Results showed that prestimulus alpha power over the occipital area directly affected visual perception, and low-frequency frontal-occipital phase synchronization predicted prestimulus occipital alpha power. These findings suggest that frontal-occipital phase synchronization can predict occipital alpha power that directly affects perceptual decision-making.
