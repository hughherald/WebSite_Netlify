abstract: Brain source localization estimates neural activity sources inside the brain from scalp EEG and MEG signals, and its millisecond-level temporal resolution provides an important complement to other neuroimaging methods. However, localization accuracy is challenged by the ill-posed inverse problem and by practical factors including recording modality, electrode number and density, and head volume conduction modeling. This review summarizes accuracy evaluation methods from both theoretical and practical perspectives, discusses key factors affecting localization precision, and introduces representative applications in basic research and clinical settings.
