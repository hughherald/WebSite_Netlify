abstract: Prestimulus alpha oscillations are associated with variability in visual contrast detection, but the causal role of alpha traveling waves has remained unclear. This study combined EEG analysis and phase-shifted transcranial alternating current stimulation (tACS) to examine the causal link between prestimulus alpha traveling waves and visual contrast detection across fatigue states. Results showed that 45-degree phase-shifted tACS reduced forward traveling waves, increased backward traveling waves, and concurrently increased visual contrast threshold compared with sham, especially under low fatigue. These state-dependent effects support a potential causal contribution of prestimulus alpha traveling waves to visual contrast detection.
